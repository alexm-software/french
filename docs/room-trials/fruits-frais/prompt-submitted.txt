Storybook ink and gouache, eye-level inside a blue-glass covered market. One shared errand, not a row of stools. Daylight from the glass roof and from a tall open arch at the back, looking onto a cobbled lane and trees. On the right, an open aisle leads into the next stall, where the edge of a picnic table shows under the same blue glass.

An adult woman stands on the left, head to feet on the cobbles. One arm holds a shopping basket. The other hand waves two red apples on short ribbons, like cheerleader pom-poms. An adult man stands on the right, head to feet, holding a bunch of fresh green grapes and turned toward her as he adds fruit to her basket.

On the oak counter between them: a pile of peaches, a pile of oranges, one pear, a pile of strawberries, and pairs of cherries on their stems. A small monkey sits by a bunch of bananas and holds one banana. The monkey is not on the man. Everyone and every fruit is well inside the frame, with bare floor around the arch and the right-hand aisle.

No letters, no knife, no child. The two people share the stall and the same purchase.
